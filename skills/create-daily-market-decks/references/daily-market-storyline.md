# Daily Market Storyline

Use this structure for a short daily market presentation:

1. **Cover**: date, latest data date, market universe.
2. **Executive Snapshot**: what moved, leaders/laggards, 1D/5D/20D table.
3. **Trend**: normalized price chart over the last 60 trading sessions.
4. **Cross-Asset Move**: bar chart of 1D/5D/20D returns.
5. **Risk Map**: 20D return versus annualized 20D volatility.
6. **Presenter Watchlist**: three concise talking points and next-session watch items.

## Talk Track Pattern

Use short, presentable statements:

- "Risk tone is mixed: equities are [up/down], while defensive assets are [up/down]."
- "[Leader] led the day; [laggard] lagged."
- "The 20D trend favors [symbol] while volatility is highest in [symbol]."
- "Watch whether the move confirms through breadth, rates, and USD-sensitive assets."

Avoid advice language such as "buy", "sell", "must allocate", or "guaranteed".

## Four-Hour Presentation Pattern

Use this mode when the user needs a long daily market session, class, seminar, or internal briefing rather than a short morning update.

Recommended structure:

1. **Opening and Agenda**: frame the session, data date, market universe, and expected discussion flow.
2. **Market Snapshot**: show the same core facts as the short deck, but pause for interpretation.
3. **How to Read the Charts**: explain the snapshot table, normalized trend chart, return bars, and risk scatter so the audience can follow the rest of the session.
4. **Asset Deep Dives**: use one chart slide plus one discussion slide for each major proxy. Keep the chart slide factual and the discussion slide facilitative.
5. **Scenario Planning**: convert signals into risk-on/risk-off cases, watch items, and next-session checks.
6. **Presentation Playbook**: close with a repeatable daily routine, wording rules, Q&A, and next steps.

For a 4-hour session, aim for about 30 slides. Use discussion slides to create time for interaction; do not try to fill 4 hours with dense charts alone.

## Premium Outlook Style Pattern

Use `--visual-style premium-outlook` when the target quality is closer to a private bank / asset manager outlook publication than a dashboard.

Design principles:

- Use a report-like sequence: cover, key takeaways, contents, introduction, chapter dividers, exhibits, implications, scenario planning, Q&A.
- Make chart pages feel like exhibits: one chart, a clear exhibit title, source line, and a side rail with presenter interpretation.
- Use editorial whitespace and fewer objects per page.
- Use warm neutral paper, bronze/teal accents, serif display headlines, thin divider rules, and restrained chart colors.
- Use premium visual panels as chapter signals, but avoid copying any third-party logo, template, image, or proprietary brand system.
- Keep all numbers and chart labels deterministic and editable through the generation script, not image-generator text.
